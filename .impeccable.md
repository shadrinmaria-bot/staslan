# Impeccable

## Design Context

Derived from the project brief and the work so far rather than from a teach
interview — correct anything that reads wrong and the design will follow it.

### Users
People who like food and read about it for pleasure: someone browsing on a
phone over coffee, or on a laptop in the evening deciding whether to cook or
to go out. They are not in a hurry and they are not looking things up in a
rush — there is no task to complete, only three pages to read. Daylight and
lamplight both, hence a light theme with a real dark counterpart.

### Brand Personality
Warm, hand-made, quietly exacting. The voice is a knowledgeable friend who
has eaten a lot of ramen and will not oversell it: short declarative
sentences, dry asides, no exclamation marks, no "delicious journey". Confident
enough to leave white space and say nothing in it.

### Aesthetic Direction
Print editorial — a well-made food quarterly, not a restaurant landing page.
Warm paper, warm ink, one vermilion accent borrowed from a lacquer bowl.
Type does the work: a Japanese mincho for display, a Japanese gothic for text.
Hairline rules, menu leader dots, numbered entries, a single raised initial
per page.

Anti-references: startup landing pages, hero gradients, card grids, glassy
panels, stock-photo smiling chefs, anything that needs a "Book now" button.

### Design Principles
1. **Type carries the hierarchy.** Size and family shift, not boxes and fills.
2. **One accent, rarely.** Vermilion marks the thing that matters on a screen
   — a number, a link, a label — and nothing else.
3. **Space is a material.** Vary it for rhythm; let sections breathe unevenly.
4. **Print references over screen references.** Leaders, plate marks,
   hairlines, folios — devices from paper, executed precisely.
5. **Quiet motion.** One staggered reveal per page load, and nothing else
   moves unless you touch it.
